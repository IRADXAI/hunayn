The Works of Hunayn ibn Ishaq

حنين بن إسحاق العبادي
Physician, translator, and scholar of the House of Wisdom · Baghdad, 809–873
Hunayn ibn Ishaq (Latin: Johannitius) was a Church of the East (Nestorian) Christian physician and the greatest translator of the Abbasid Golden Age, rendering Galen, Hippocrates, Plato, and Aristotle from Greek into Syriac and Arabic. He was said to be paid in gold by the weight of his manuscripts. He is credited with more than 100 original works, though only a fraction survive today. What follows are his principal surviving works and the best places to read them, favoring public-domain and open digital editions.

Original Medical Works

The Book of the Ten Treatises on the Eye Public Domain
Kitāb al-ʿashr maqālāt fī al-ʿayn · Internet Archive · Meyerhof edition, 1928
The earliest known systematic textbook of ophthalmology. Full Arabic text with facing English translation and glossary. Covers the anatomy of the eye, its diseases, and their treatment, including cataract and corneal surgery.
Ten Treatises on the Eye — Wellcome Collection edition Public Domain
Wellcome Collection · 2 volumes online · Public Domain Mark
A second freely readable digitization of the Meyerhof edition, including his biographical essay "The Life of Hunain ibn Is-haq." High-quality page scans.
Questions on Medicine (Isagoge / Masāʾil fī al-ṭibb)
Qatar Digital Library · manuscript images and scholarly essay
His question-and-answer introduction to Galenic medicine. Translated into Latin by Constantine the African as the Isagoge Johannitii, it became the opening text of the Articella, the first medical curriculum of medieval European universities.
Isagoge ad Tegni Galeni (Johannitius) — Latin tradition
Wellcome Collection · medieval manuscript fragment
The Latin form of Questions on Medicine as studied in Paris, Bologna, Oxford, Montpellier, and Padua into the sixteenth century. This entry shows a fourteenth-century glossed manuscript.
Questions on Medicine for Students — oldest Syriac version
Transcription & translation of Vat. Syr. 192
A modern scholarly edition of the earliest surviving Syriac copy, notable because Hunayn wrote only the opening portion before his death and his nephew Hubaysh completed it.

On His Translations

Hunayn ibn Ishaq on His Galen Translations (Risāla)
Parallel Arabic–English · ed. & trans. John C. Lamoreaux, BYU Press, 2016
His famous epistle to ʿAli ibn Yahya cataloguing which of Galen's books he had translated, for whom, and into what language. The single most important source on the translation movement of the Abbasid era.

Note on lost works: ancient sources credit Hunayn with over a hundred titles — on the pulse, fevers, urine, diet, hygiene, veterinary medicine, colors, a Greek–Syriac dictionary, an Arabic grammar "according to the system of the Greeks," a universal history, and a Risāla on the misfortunes of his own life. Most do not survive; where they do, they are scattered across manuscript libraries in Istanbul, the Vatican, Cairo, and London rather than in a single archive.
